Abstract
This paper proposes a measurement specification and reference routing rule linking Thought
Trajectory Space (TTS) to Cognitive Transition Architecture (CTA). TTS records abstraction,
inferential dependency depth, and metacognitive review while retaining semantic content,
evidence, goals, and history outside the three observation coordinates. The specification fixes
abstraction boundaries, proposition and dependency units, multi-conclusion summaries, and
distinct notions of content control. Categorical transitions are the default; geometric analysis
and activation steering require additional measurement assumptions. The reference router
combines goal-related utility and cost with Jensen-Shannon divergence between operator-
conditioned realized transition distributions and an expert transition prior. Unsupported
observations trigger exact fallback to the same baseline, without modifying contract permissions.
A conditional bound limits changes in the router’s surrogate utility, not in true task performance.
Constructed numerical cases illustrate changed choices, fallback, and failure under a misspecified
prior. A small reference selector and its numerical and branching unit tests were executed.
No LLM-integrated CTA, annotation reliability study, activation intervention, or model
performance and learning-efficiency experiment was conducted. The evaluation separates
native operator contribution from the incremental value of TTS, holds the TTS signal path
fixed across a factorial comparison, and includes budget-matched alternative representations.
The resulting specification supports falsifiable tests of the separate contributions of observation,
routing, and contract enforcement; their empirical utility remains to be evaluated.
Keywords: thought trajectory space, cognitive transition architecture, construct validity, operator
selection, executable contracts, categorical transitions, learning efficiency
1. Introduction
Some problem-solving processes begin with concrete observations, derive a general structure, and
return to testable cases. Others repeat the same representations without checking their premises,
or perform valid intermediate steps yet arrive at a result that does not address the original goal.
Describing these differences requires specifying what changes and what remains invariant during
reasoning, rather than considering only the correctness of the final answer. This study takes the
expression “direction of thought” as a starting point and formulates it as a measurable research
question.
Representing cognitive content in geometric spaces, distinguishing abstract from concrete repre-
sentations, and analyzing neural population activity as dynamics each have established research
backgrounds [1–3]. In large language models (LLMs), researchers have also investigated output
control using activation directions and the analysis of reasoning trajectories [6,7,14–17]. Con-
sequently, this study does not claim that representing reasoning with vectors and trajectories is
itself a new discovery.

The paper considers four questions. First, can the structural properties of reasoning steps be
consistently encoded in human-interpretable coordinates? Second, do changes in those coordinates
provide information beyond content similarity or simple step indices? Third, can using valid
transition patterns for control reduce the number of training examples or the execution cost
required for the same performance? Fourth, can incorporating valid operational relations into an
architecture reduce part of the burden of repeatedly learning desired procedures? These questions
require different levels of evidence: measurement, prediction, intervention, and execution structure.
Thought Trajectory Space (TTS) is proposed as a conceptual framework for addressing these
questions together. Its central design separates the coordinates of a state from the relations
between states. Abstraction, dependency depth, and metacognitive review are properties observed
at an individual step; continuity, changes of direction, and evidence links are evaluated between
steps. Intuitive and analytical processing are not placed on an axis opposed to concreteness and
abstraction. Nor are the three coordinates assumed to contain all semantic information or fully
determine the next state.
The contributions are limited to four design artifacts.C1 is a measurement specification covering
A/D/M coding procedures, content controls, and conditions for using the scales. C2 is an
observation-control interface distinguishing text, activations, and categorical transitions.C3
consists of CTA contracts for operator selection, execution, and result acceptance, together with
an explicit reference routing rule that uses TTS information within those contracts.C4 is an
evaluation protocol that decomposes the standalone and combined effects of the components and
their data, computation, and construction costs. Numerical and branching tests of the reference
selector are a limited implementation check of C3, not evidence of the empirical utility of these
four contributions.
The motivation for connecting TTS and CTA is to address both the induction of particular
reasoning behaviors and the definition of the computational relations required for those behaviors
as components. Discovering interpretable coordinates does not, however, by itself construct
corresponding semantic operators. Sections 3-5 therefore address observation and guidance,
Section 6 addresses execution structure, and the conditional propositions in Section 7 and the
hypotheses and evaluation design in Sections 9-10 examine their connection.
2. Theoretical Background and Related Work
2.1. Conceptual spaces, abstraction, and dynamics
Gärdenfors’s conceptual spaces describe conceptual representations as geometric structures with
multiplequalitydimensions[1]. TTSdrawsontheperspectiveofassigninginterpretablecoordinates
to cognitive representations. It nevertheless distinguishes a space describing what a concept is
from a space describing the form of reasoning applied to that concept. For the same “network
error,” for example, a step may examine a particular log, describe a general failure structure, or
review the criteria used to make a judgment.
The concrete-abstract distinction in construal-level theory provides a conceptual background for
A [2]. The A proposed here codes how strongly a task representation is tied to an instance or
generalized beyond it; it is not a measure that transfers the full psychological-distance effects of
construal-level theory to LLMs. Empirical support for the existing cognitive theory therefore does
not establish the validity of the TTS coordinate.
Research on neural population dynamics concerns the temporal structure of high-dimensional
neural activity and its relation to computation [3]. This perspective motivates analyzing transitions
as well as individual states. State spaces derived from neural activity, LLM activation spaces,
and human-annotated reasoning spaces are nevertheless different observation spaces. Their
correspondence requires separate data.

2.2. Processing modes and metacognitive review
The distinction between autonomous and controlled processing in dual-process research is not
the same as the distinction between abstractness and concreteness [4]. TTS does not identify
an intuitive transition merely from a high A label or a large jump. The absence of recorded
intermediate steps is likewise not interpreted as showing that an internal process was omitted.
Processing mode is recorded as an additional attribute only when there is separate observational
evidence.
Research on measuring metacognition distinguishes confidence, performance accuracy, and the
correspondence between accuracy and confidence [5]. M does not measure this entire metacognitive
ability. Instead, M denotes the level of the object under review: whether the current utterance
or action concerns the problem itself, the grounds for reasoning, or the appropriateness of the
evaluation criteria. The magnitude of M and the success of the review are evaluated separately.
2.3. Interpretable representations and activation steering
Activation Addition intervenes on model outputs using activation differences between contrasting
inputs [6]. Representation Engineering organizes approaches to observing and manipulating
population-level representations rather than only individual neurons [7]. These studies provide
methodological grounds for exploring meaningful representation directions; they do not show that
each TTS coordinate A/D/M exists as a stable, single linear direction.
Concept Bottleneck Models use human-specified concepts as intermediate representations in
prediction [8]. Their connection to TTS is the explicit assignment of meaning to interpretable
variables and the question of whether those variables can be intervened on. The present proposal
does not, however, make a bottleneck that computes the final answer exclusively from the three
coordinates its default architecture. Content and context remain in high-dimensional states or
external records, while A/D/M serve as auxiliary observation and control variables.
High predictive performance by a probe must be distinguished from the original model’s actual
use of that information in computation. Hewitt and Liang proposed control tasks for isolating task
learning and memorization by probes [10]. TTS likewise requires controls for random labels, surface
form, and step position, together with intervention experiments. Model-generated explanations
may also fail to faithfully expose the original computation; text annotations are therefore not
equated directly with observations of internal thought [9].
2.4. Reasoning trajectories, latent reasoning, and adaptive control
Coconut proposes continuous latent reasoning that reuses the final hidden state as the next
input representation [11]. Bias-Only Adaptation steers reasoning by learning a limited number of
additional parameters [12]. Unlike these two approaches, Thinking Intervention insertstextual
intervention informationat specified positions during reasoning [13, Section 4]. Reference [13]
is thus cited as prior work on textual intervention in reasoning, not as evidence of direct hidden-
activation manipulation. None of these studies is used as validation of A/D/M or of the efficiency
of the present combination.
Sun and colleagues analyze stepwise representation-space structure, differences between correct and
incorrect paths, and trajectory-based intervention [14]. CTRLS addresses reasoning search using
latent-state transitions [15], and RISER uses a router to select and combine reusable reasoning
vectors for intervention [16]. RISER also addresses interpretable cognitive functions and control;
it would therefore be inappropriate to contrast TTS with a claim that all existing methods use
only uninterpretable coordinates.
A*-Thought-V2, released in September 2026, analyzes local transitions and alignment with
the question-to-solution direction in a three-dimensional PCA space and combines explicit and

2.5. Cognitive architectures, modular computation, and execution constraints
Organizing problem solving around states, operators, and goals already occurs in cognitive
architectures. Soar distinguishes operator selection and application to states, working memory,
goals, and the construction of subproblems in response to impasses [19]. The broad idea of
designing a graph of reasoning operators is therefore not claimed as originating in this study.
Neural Programmer-Interpreters combine a trainable execution core, program memory, and
domain-specific encoders; Neural Module Networks compose learned modules according to input
structure [20,21]. These are prior examples showing that functional composition and neural learning
can coexist. The additional question for CTA concerns the effects of specifying input-output
relations, acceptance conditions, and links to TTS observations, rather than merely assigning
names to modules.
Differentiable Convex Optimization Layers incorporate a well-defined optimization problem
as a layer of a neural network [22]. This is a direct example of defining a layer as a particular
computational problem rather than only a generic weighted transformation. Abstract interpretation
also addresses correspondences between concrete and abstract states and sound approximation [25].
Section 6 uses these principles in restricted formal-domain examples. It does not claim that every
abstraction or verification operation in natural language reduces to convex optimization or static
analysis.
Shielding research, which blocks specification-violating actions proposed by a learned policy,
separates execution constraints from performance optimization [23]. Its guarantees depend on

the specified requirements and system model. CTA similarly distinguishes calling a verifier,
satisfying a defined relation, and solving the user’s actual problem. Recent SAE-based reasoning
steering intervenes on reasoning strategies through disentangled representation features [24], but
an intervention effect is not itself a guarantee that a given contract is satisfied on every execution.
The candidate distinction of this design thus lies not in cognitive architectures, modularity, or hard
constraints individually, but in combining the TTS observation language with operator contracts
and separately evaluating the incremental value of that combination. Literature comparison alone
does not establish that the combination is novel or effective.
2.6. Direct comparison with integrated language-agent structures
CoALA organizes language agents around modular memory, internal and external action spaces,
and a generalized decision-making procedure [26]. CTA’s working-memory, operator, and controller
outline is therefore insufficient by itself to constitute an architecture newer than CoALA. The
additional specification here fixes what is observed within such a structure, which numerical
rule changes operator priorities, and which permissions those observations cannot affect. These
components could also be added to a CoALA-style implementation; the proposal is therefore a
testable component design rather than a mutually exclusive replacement.
Formal-LLM represents requirements as automata and generates natural-language plans under
those constraints [27]. Section 4.1 of that paper distinguishes plan validity and executability
from performance and optimality. CTA retains this distinction. The additional candidates
specified here are version binding between accepted states and checked objects, final-output
obligations, and selection among admissible operators using categorical transition distributions.
Their specification does not imply that an extended Formal-LLM implementation could not provide
the same functions.
Tree of Thoughts generates and evaluates intermediate thoughts and organizes search and back-
tracking [28]. Graph of Thoughts includes thought-dependency graphs, transformations, evaluation,
ranking, and a controller, distinguishing a graph of operations from dynamically maintained rea-
soning states [29, Sections 3-4]. Using graphs, transformations, and evaluators is therefore not
a novelty claim either. The discriminating question is the incremental information A/D/M
transitions provide for selection when content, operator state, and budget are held constant.
Agent Workflow Memory induces reusable workflows from experience and selectively supplies them
to subsequent actions [30]. The reuse-efficiency hypothesis H8 must also be compared with such
external procedural memory. Benefits of reuse alone are not attributed to the internal operator
structure or to TTS.

The candidate distinction is not merely that interpretable coordinates exist. It is the joint provision
of a fixed codebook, a sample space of realized transitions, a distribution-comparison selection
term, exact baseline fallback, and an ablation that removes that term. Jensen-Shannon divergence,
weighted objectives, and hard masking are not claimed as new mathematics. A narrative literature

3. Defining Thought Trajectory Space
3.1. Observation units and context
A reasoning record is represented as semantically segmented stepsi = 1,...,n. A step is not
necessarily a sentence or a token. Initial annotation places boundaries where the dominant
function changes, for example from presenting an observation to making an inference, generating a
hypothesis, evaluating evidence, or reviewing a decision criterion. A sentence containing multiple
functions may be segmented or assigned multiple labels.

The zi in Eq. (1) contains selected observational properties, not an entire mental state or the

principle. Vocabulary difficulty, length, and vagueness are not coding criteria. The stated scope
and explicit parameterization in the original record provide the evidence.

3.3. D: Inferential dependency depth
D records how many layers of support dependencies connect the active conclusion to premises
explicitly stated in the record. It is distinguished from the number of reasoning steps, elapsed
time, number of hypotheses, and general reasoning ability. Rules for constructing the graph from
the record are fixed before depth is computed.
Node unit. A node is a proposition whose truth or acceptance can be assessed independently.
Independent conjuncts are separated, but a sentence is retained intact when splitting it would
change its conditions or quantifier scope. A restatement with the same meaning and provenance
status is recorded as an alias of an existing node. Claims updated with new evidence receive
separate versions; repetition alone does not create additional depth.
Edge unit. One explicit act of deriving a conclusion from a premise set is stored as an inference
record. Multiplepremisesusedjointlysharean inference_idand parent_set; depthcomputation
uses edges from all parents to the conclusion. A rule object is linked as a justification for the
inference, but applying a rule does not create an artificial intermediate conclusion that increases
depth again. Annotators do not supply unrecorded intermediate proofs. If several omitted steps
are compressed into one sentence, the record retains one derivation relation and separately marks
the possibility of omission.

3.4. M: Metacognitive review level
M denotes the current object of review. M0 concerns the object-level problem, M1 the grounds,
assumptions, or uncertainty of the current reasoning, and M2 the appropriateness of goals,
evaluation criteria, or verification procedures. If the distinction between M1 and M2 is not
reproducible, reliability and confusion patterns are reported and a merged codebook is evaluated
separately. Results from a modified codebook are not retroactively counted as success of this
three-level specification.
The phrase “I will check again” alone does not raise M. The assumption or ground being checked,
and the reason for checking it, must be identifiable. Announcing a check, performing it, and
incorporating its result into a revised conclusion receive separate action labels. M describes the
level of review; whether that review was accurate or timely is a separate quality variable.
M is assigned according to the review target. “Does evidence E1 actually support this conclusion?”
is M1, whereas “Is the current evidential criterion sufficient to assess this goal?” is M2. The mere
presence of a goal in the task input does not warrant M2: a statement evaluating the adequacy of
the criterion is required. A concerns representational generalization and M the review target; a
criterion review confined to one case can therefore be A1 and M2 simultaneously. M2 does not
automatically imply A4.

comparisons. Distance- and angle-based analyses are undertaken only after assessing whether
appropriate numerical coding can be established using anchor examples and contrast ratings.

3.6. Categorical reference representation and segmentation versions
The reference selector uses A0-A4, six bins for raw D (0, 1, 2, 3, 4, and 5 or greater), and M0-M2.
No numerical distance is assigned to these 90 combinations. The D bins are a convention for
controlling variance and sparsity in this reference method, and raw graph depth must also be
preserved. Results using other bins are reported as different configurations.
When operator execution records are available, a step boundary is the return of one completed
operation. With text-only records, it is the smallest meaning unit with one dominant function
and active conclusion. Compound or ambiguous units are not forcibly split, and source offsets
and segmentation versions are fixed. Data with different execution and textual boundaries are
analyzed in separate strata. Earlier boundaries are not retrospectively redefined after reading
future outcomes.

4.2. Semantic connection and inferential warrant
Semantic connectednessCs(i) describes the relatedness of the content at stepsi and i + 1. When
embedding similarity is used, the embedding’s suitability for the domain and language is assessed
separately. Similarity is one measure of content relations, not a logical-entailment checker.

Inferential warrantCi(i) assesses how well the next step is supported given the transition type,
explicit premises, and available evidence. Deduction, inductive generalization, causal-hypothesis
proposal, and evidence-gathering actions have different requirements and are not reduced to a single
entailment judgment. Initial coding uses “supported,” “conditionally supported,” “insufficient
grounds,” “contradiction,” and “not applicable.”
Analogical correspondenceCa(i) is recorded separately. Software verification and judicial evidence
assessment may share structural similarities, but a conclusion in one domain is not thereby true
in the other. Analogy can support hypothesis generation or comparison; justification in the target
domain is evaluated using that domain’s evidence. This distinction prevents treating analogy as
erroneous merely because semantic distance is large, or treating a conclusion as established merely
because the structures resemble one another.
4.3. Processing mode, speed, and measurement uncertainty
Intuitive, analytical, and mixed processing are optional auxiliary values ofµi. For humans, these
labels require separate evidence such as self-reports, response times, or task manipulations. For
LLMs, output length or a single jump does not establish a process equivalent to human intuition.

5.6. Conditions for connecting guidance to execution structure
The intervention in Eq. (14) changes an existing model’s computation to induce particular
behavior. CTA, described next, goes further by specifying which operations are admissible and
which outputs can be accepted. The two approaches are alternatives that can also be combined.
TTS supplies observation and control signals suggesting which operation to consider next; CTA
governs execution and acceptance of the chosen operation’s result.
This connection is not assumed to be an automatic equivalence. The same A/D/M change
can arise from different operations, and one operation can change the coordinates differently in
different contexts. Operator-specific transition distributions must therefore be observed to assess
correspondence between predictions and actual execution. A device that checks only increases in
coordinates is not a layer implementing a semantic operation.

6.1. Separating computational states from interpretable coordinates

rule guaranteeing a benefit.
6.4.4. Collecting effect data and specifying the estimand
Records separately retain the pre-execution state, admissible set, actual operator ID and assignment
probability, candidate state, contract verdict, post-acceptance coordinates, checking and recovery
costs, and independent goal judgment under the continuation policy. The effect model learns from
executions including rejection, undetermined outcomes, and stopping, rather than only successful
candidates. Conditional categorical likelihood is the training objective, with a fixed-capacity
multinomial classifier as the primary baseline. Task-family frequency estimation is a simple
comparator in formal tasks with sufficient data.
The primary design for causal interpretation of operator effects clones the same pre-execution
state within the supported domain and randomly assigns admissible operators. It requires positive
assignment probability for each state-operator pair, the same continuation policy, and consistent
outcome recording. Tasks with irreversible external effects are excluded unless evaluated in a
sandbox or under separate experimental consent and design. The effect is restricted to supported
states and the specified continuation policy.
Exploratory analyses using only observational logs of an existing policy report selection bias and
support gaps and do not callPν,oa causal effect. Unless the experiment manipulates A/D/M
labels themselves, the effect of an operator intervention is not the independent causal effect of
A/D/M. The expert distribution and utility/effect models are frozen without access to test results,
and future correct paths of test problems are not used as prior information.

solely to the learned policy. Deterministic computation with fixed weights and inputs, sampling
the next output, uncertainty in knowledge or evidence, and prohibition of a transition are distinct
issues. Computing a probability distribution does not necessarily require random sampling, and a
deterministic output can still be wrong.

6.8. Reduced fine-tuning and redistribution of learning responsibilities
In CTA, the designer can define some procedural admissibility, explicit rule application, and
protected state updates. Learning may still determine how input is parsed into semantic structure,
which hypothesis is promising, which abstraction suits the goal, and how modules are composed.
An operation’s specification is distinct from the knowledge needed to apply it.
The question “Does fine-tuning become meaningless?” thus becomes testable in a more specific
form. When predefined operations are reused, can a new task reach target performance by adapting
representation interfaces, operator selection, or particular modules, without retuning the full
model? Alternatively, can the same contracts be maintained without learning repeated examples
of procedural compliance? Both are possible research hypotheses; neither requires eliminating all
learning of knowledge, representations, or operations in every domain.
If additional operators or routers are optimized while base weights remain frozen, their training
costs are still included. If all modules are frozen and only graph configuration changes, human
configuration effort and search costs are recorded separately. Comparisons between a native
architecture trained from scratch and a pretrained-model-based approach also separate the
asymmetry in pretraining costs.
7. Conditional Propositions of the Design
The propositions in this section are elementary mathematical properties derived from definitions
and assumptions. They do not report the observation of a new cognitive phenomenon or empirical

7.2. Proposition 2: Content-insensitive structural coordinates are not sufficient
for correctness
If A/D/M coding retains only structural levels of reasoning while maintaining the truth or falsity
of specific factual content separately, true and false statements with the same coordinates can
be constructed. For example, only one of two observational claims with identical structure and
abstraction level may agree with the source data.
Therefore,zi or its trajectory alone cannot generally be guaranteed to determine correctness across
all tasks. TTS must preserve evidence, content, and goals, and trajectory-based predictions must
be evaluated as additional signals. In particular, “all intermediate steps are warranted” and “the
user’s goal is satisfied” are separate judgments: a valid procedure may solve a different goal.
7.3. Proposition 3: A prior that increases the probability of valid transitions
can reduce local search
For a fixed context, letV+ be the set of valid candidate transitions, and letp0 and pE be the
probability masses assigned to this set by the baseline and expert policies, respectively. Under the
simplifying assumptions of independent candidate proposals and exact judgments, the mixture in
Eq. (17) has the following success probability:
pλ= (1−λ)p0 +λpE. (35)
If pE >p 0 and λ>0, thenpλ>p 0. Afterm independent candidate proposals, the probability of
obtaining at least one valid candidate is1−(1−pλ)m. Whenpλ> 0, the expected number of
proposals until the first success is1/pλ.
This proposition identifies conditions under which valid prior information can help. Actual
multistep reasoning involves changing states, dependent candidates, and imperfect judgments, so
the result is not directly extended to the complexity of entire reasoning processes or the rate of
reduction in training data. Whether a locally valid transition advances the overall goal is also a
separate question. Moreover, if the unit cost of candidate generation and checking or the cost of
constructing the prior increases, fewer proposals may still entail a higher total cost.

Consider a set of formal premisesΓ, a formalized goalgF, and a candidate proofπ. Assume

9. Falsifiable Research Hypotheses
TTS validation is not reduced to a single overall success criterion. Reproducible coordinate
annotation, decoding coordinates from activations, and improving performance by controlling
coordinates support different hypotheses.
H1. Measurability of coordinates.A and M ratings and D dependency-structure annotations
are reproducible across independent evaluators and cannot be explained solely by changes in topic,
length, or particular expressions. If distinctions between axes are not reproducible or provide no
additional information over a two-axis model, the three-axis specification is revised.
H2. Additional information in activations. A fixed-capacity probe predicts A/D/M
annotations in unseen task families and provides information beyond controls using only token
count, step position, keywords, and content representations. If its effect is indistinguishable from
random-label or surface-form controls, the activation interpretation is not supported.
H3. Predictive value of trajectories.At a fixed observation point before the final answer is
seen, TTS transition features improve prediction of error or goal deviation beyond current content,
step count, cost, and existing activation features. A state-coordinate-only model is compared with
one adding transition features to identify the distinct contribution of differences and directional
analysis.
H4. Effectiveness of context-conditioned intervention.Under the same execution budget,
TTS control improves task performance or reduces total execution cost while maintaining accuracy
relative to no intervention, a simple verification prompt, random directions, static steering, and
context-unconditioned steering. If only coordinate scores or verification-related expressions increase
without improved actual outcomes, the control hypothesis is not supported.
H5. Efficiency contribution of expert prior information.With the same starting model and
supervision budget, expert transition priors reduce the number of training examples or amount of
training computation needed to reach target performance relative to a method without directional
information. Economic efficiency including construction, annotation, and search costs is evaluated
separately. A reduction in output tokens alone is interpreted as a partial result on inference cost,
not as support for H5.
H6. Implementation conformance of execution contracts.Within a defined threat and
fault model, a CTA implementation blocks contract bypasses, reuse of stale evidence, replacement
of the checked target, treatment of indeterminate judgments as passes, and direct output bypasses.
A single reproducible acceptance bypass within the declared supported model refutes the imple-
mentation’s guarantee. An absence of observed bypasses in testing is not reported as a proof of
Proposition 4 across all implementation states.
H7a. Functional contribution of native operators.Under the same tasks, supervision,
and execution budgets, native CTA operators produce target-operation effects distinct from mere
role labels, generic blocks, and external control, and downstream computation actually uses their
results. Removal, replacement, and interventions on intermediate results test this hypothesis.
H7b. Conditional incremental value of combining TTS with CTA.With the same
CTA, baseline-utility model, and continuation policy, adding the TTS selection term of Section
6.4 provides a prespecified minimum benefit in goal performance or cost relative to removing it.
Comparisons also include alternative representations and direct operator-state labels matched in
supervision, capacity, and cost. B3 minus B2 is the primary comparison; B3 minus B1 separately
asks whether the combination exceeds TTS alone. Both comparisons must succeed to claim
superiority over both individual components. Positive superadditive interaction is treated only as
a separate hypothesis. Benefits attributable solely to internal constraints are not credited to TTS.
H8. Adaptation efficiency through operator reuse. Given the same initial assets and
data access, a CTA that reuses an operator library reduces the additional training examples or
computation needed to reach target performance on new tasks relative to baselines that readjust
26

PAGE 27
Thought Trajectory Space v1.0
the entire model or its modules. Adaptation using only frozen modules is distinguished from
additional training of routers or operators. This is an efficiency hypothesis for prespecified
transfer-task families, not a universal claim that fine-tuning is unnecessary in all domains.
The scope of failure is also separated. Even without support for H4, the observation language
in H1 may remain useful. H1 holding only in some tasks does not imply that cognition in its
entirety cannot be represented by coordinates. Conversely, success in one task is not generalized
to a universal structure of humans or all models. Procedural conformance under H6 may hold
without the semantic effects of H7 or the efficiency gains of H8. If the combination effect in H7 is
absent, the practical basis for combining TTS and CTA weakens accordingly.
10. Staged Evaluation Design
This section specifies planned evaluations of LLMs, annotation, and architecture. No data
collection, LLM execution or training experiment, confirmatory statistical test, or activation
intervention has been conducted. These plans are distinct from the reference-selector unit tests in
Section 8.4.
10.1. Stage I: Annotatability and construct validity
Three domains are used: logic tasks with explicit rules, code and debugging tasks with checkable
test results, and evidence-assessment tasks restricted to supplied materials. An illustrative initial
pilot comprises 100 original problems per domain, two open-weight models, and three generations
per problem, for a total of 1,800 traces. This is an example of study scale, not a sample size
with guaranteed statistical power. Confirmatory sample size is determined using pilot variance,
within-cluster correlation, and a predefined smallest effect of interest.
Where possible, annotators assess step boundaries, A/M categories, D dependency edges, transition
types, and grounds without seeing final-answer correctness or model identity. At least part of
the material receives independent duplicate annotation, with pre-consensus agreement reported
separately. Reporting only final consensus labels must not conceal disagreement.
Label distributions, confusion matrices, ordinal agreement, and dependency-edge agreement are
reported for each axis. Contrastive material evaluates how well A is distinguished from character
count, M from the word “verification,” and D from sentence index. Q-exact and Base-fixed cases
are reported in separate strata, along with A2/A3 and A3/A4 confusion and missing-label rates
in compound units. For D, parent-set/edge F1 and depth agreement are evaluated separately
on aligned nodes. Robustness to changes in transition boundaries is also checked. When human
ratings do not reproducibly distinguish the categories, activation interpretation is not used to
claim success at the next stage.
10.2. Stage II: Representation correspondence and prediction
Training, validation, and test splits are organized by original problem and generation-template
lineage, not individual sentences. Three generations of the same problem, translations, and
paraphrases must not enter different splits. Coordinate transformations, probes, metrics, and
intervention layers are selected entirely within training and validation splits. Final testing is
performed once on separate task families or a held-out external domain.
Categorical coordinates are evaluated with class-imbalance-aware performance and calibration
measures; D is evaluated with both rank correlation and prediction error. A strong text baseline
that reads content is included to test whether activations provide an advantage. Probe capacity and
training budgets are matched to controls, and label-shuffled control tasks assess memorization [10].

Final-error prediction uses only prefix traces available at an operationally known fixed token budget
or a predetermined number of steps. Observation points such as “half of the final length,” which
require knowledge of the future total length, are designated retrospective analyses. The treatment
and inclusion rate of early-terminated traces are reported to avoid selecting only surviving long
paths. AUROC, AUPRC, calibration error, and usefulness at actionable intervention points are
compared before and after adding TTS.
10.3. Stage III: Causal evaluation of interventions
Intervention conditions are randomly assigned under the same problem, initial context, sampling
settings, and execution budget. Baseline controls include no intervention, an equally long general
verification instruction, a random activation direction, a static direction matched in magnitude to
TTS, and a content-only controller. Where implementation and resources permit, comparisons
also include existing trajectory control and adaptive vector-combination methods [14,16]. Direct
performance comparisons with methods that have not actually been implemented are not claimed.
Ablations remove one of A, D, or M; use only current coordinates without transition directions; or
shuffle expert labels across contexts. They distinguish effects of the axis definitions from those of
additional supervision or computation introduced by an extra controller.
Post-intervention evaluation uses correctness, test passage, actual use of evidence, and goal
satisfaction rather than an increase in the original probe’s scores. The rate at which intervention
damages an already correct path is also reported, together with unnecessary repeated verification,
increased tool calls, premature termination, and reduced diversity of valid paths.
10.4. Stage IV: Separating training requirements from inference cost
Training-data efficiency is compared on nested subsets sharing the same initial checkpoint, data
pool, and annotation source. The number of examples needed by a method to reach target
accuracy a can be defined as
N∗(a) = inf{N : Acc(N)≥a}. (39)
If the target is not reached within the observed range, this is reported without arbitrary extrap-
olation. Equal example counts need not imply equal amounts of repeated training, so learning
curves are also reported for training tokens, optimization steps, computation, or GPU time.
Inference efficiency is compared at equal accuracy or within a predefined noninferiority margin
using input/output tokens, latent steps, tool calls, retries, and latency.

10.5. Statistical analysis and preregistration
For each primary hypothesis, the primary metric, smallest effect of interest, comparison groups,
stopping criteria, exclusions, and multiple-comparison procedure are specified before confirmatory
testing. Thresholds are not changed after observing test results. Multiple traces from the
same original problem are not counted as independent samples; uncertainty is estimated using a
problem-level cluster bootstrap or an appropriate hierarchical model.
Effect sizes and confidence intervals are reported, not only p-values. Claims of “lower cost at the
same accuracy” require both an accuracy-noninferiority criterion and uncertainty estimates for
cost reduction. Low inter-annotator agreement or insufficient samples are reported as limitations
rather than converted into definitive success or failure.
Appendix C lists the required preregistration items and hypothesis-specific decision conditions.
This evaluation plan has not been externally preregistered.

11. Discussion
11.1. The meaning of human-like reasoning directions
In this framework, “human-like reasoning directions” refers to transition structures in observable
problem-solving strategies. Examples include structuring conditions, seeking evidence that
distinguishes hypotheses, reviewing judgment criteria, and returning to concrete checks. It
does not assert equivalence between LLM computation and human experience, consciousness, or
internal neural computation.
Even if useful coordinates are found in LLMs, whether they have the same meaning and measure-
ment units in human data requires separate validation. Future analyses of human records should
collect actions, evidence use, and revision histories for the same tasks and evaluate correspondence
between self-report and actual performance. No human-participant study was conducted for this
paper.
11.2. Task appropriateness rather than a universal ideal trajectory
Treating high A, D, and M as a universal objective can induce repeated generalization and
rechecking even for simple tasks. Conversely, repeated concrete operations may be the essential
process in a computationally difficult task. A good direction should be defined by whether the
next step is valid relative to the current goal, evidence, and cost, not by absolute coordinate
values.

Local transition validity and overall goal satisfaction are also separate. A controller must therefore
maintain the goal to be solved and its completion conditions in addition to a signal that “the next
step looks plausible.” This is particularly important when connecting TTS to agent execution.
Trajectory information can be an auxiliary observation for planning, execution, and verification,
but it does not replace actual artifacts or evidence of completion.
11.3. Scope and remaining technical problems
Key technical issues include reproducible segmentation, numerical calibration of ordinal ratings,
distinguishing D from M, aligning axes across models and languages, and relating observation
directions to causal intervention directions. If these are unstable, categorical transition models or
task-specific coordinates may be more appropriate than retaining a complete three-dimensional
geometry.
The paper distinguishes control information observable only in external materials or output traces
from information obtained through internal activations. For models without internal access,
text/action-based external control can be tested, but its effects cannot be reported as activation-
steering effects. Likewise, the design possibility of integration with latent reasoning models is
distinct from a demonstrated reduction in training cost.
11.4. Changing the model definition and the scope of that change
The strongest interpretation of CTA includes operator relations and composition rules in the
model definition, rather than merely encouraging the behavior of trained layers after the fact.
This perspective combines learned input-reading functions with functions that execute actual rules
and constraints in a single computational structure. The central issue is a shift in the boundary
between learning and design, not the elimination of either.
Whether this structure is more efficient depends on implementation cost and task properties. In
domains that are difficult to specify, additional structure can lock in an inappropriate abstraction.
In domains that repeatedly use explicit rules, executing those rules rather than relearning them
may be advantageous. Goal performance and transfer cost under equal resources determine which
case applies, not the names of layers or the human-likeness of their descriptions.
If an external executor already provides sufficient contract conformance and goal performance,
the need for a new native architecture remains a separate issue. Conversely, benefits from native
modularity do not establish an additional contribution of observation coordinates unless they
exceed those of a modular structure without TTS. Maintaining this comparison prevents an
observation theory from being used to justify a new model without the necessary evidence.
11.5. Responsible interpretation and data handling
Using unvalidated TTS scores to assess human intelligence, personality, or mental state is outside
the scope of this research. Human records require separate procedures addressing collection
purpose, consent, anonymization, and exclusion of confidential workplace information. The present
examples are constructed for explanation; real company logs or sensitive personal reasoning records
are not released as data.
Making control outcomes appear more explainable is different from improving actual evidence
use. Public results should therefore include failures and adverse intervention effects and, where
possible, records that reproduce control choices and the evidence used.

12. Conclusion
This paper proposes a TTS-CTA specification connecting structural observations of reasoning
to execution contracts. TTS defines an A/D/M codebook and procedures for propositions,
dependencies, and content control while preserving content, evidence, and goals separately. CTA
does not substitute these observations for complete computational state; it distinguishes operator
selection, execution, result acceptance, and final output conditions.
The reference routing rule combines goal-related surrogate utility with the fit of operator-
conditioned categorical transition distributions to an expert distribution. When applicability
cannot be established, it falls back to the same baseline utility and tie-breaking rule; the selection
term cannot change permissions or contracts. Under explicit conditions, the range of surrogate
utility that this term can alter is derived. This is a mathematical property of the selector, not
noninferiority of actual performance.
Directly defining operator relations in restricted formal domains permits some procedural con-
formance and rule execution to be specified structurally. Representation extraction, operator
selection, domain knowledge, and adaptation remain possible learning targets. Whether this
allocation benefits goal performance or training and execution costs must be evaluated separately
through native-operator functionality, the incremental value of the TTS selection term, and
efficiency from operator reuse.
The results provided are measurement and execution specifications, conditional propositions,
constructed examples, and checks of a limited reference selector. A/D/M construct validity,
activation correspondence, intervention effects, and whole-system efficiency remain subjects for
empirical evaluation. The proposed comparisons separate the effects of TTS and CTA and test
whether alternative representations with the same information, supervision, and computational
budgets can replace those effects.

A.1. Coordinate transformations and metrics
If T is invertible andv′=Tv, w′=Tw, andW′=T−TWT−1, then
(v′)TW′w′=vTT TT−TWT−1Tw =vTWw. (A1)
Thus, the inner product, the norm induced by that inner product, and the cosine angle are
preserved. In contrast, retainingW′=I gives (v′)Tw′=vTT TTw, which generally differs from
the original Euclidean inner product. Preserving both lengths and angles requiresT TT =I. A
positive scalar multiple of an orthogonal transformation preserves angles but changes absolute
lengths.
This does not mean that all monotone transformations of ordinal ratings are geometrically
equivalent. Encoding A0-A4 as 0, 1, 2, 3, 4 or as 0, 1, 2, 3, 20 preserves the same order
but produces different distances. Mathematical consistency of a coordinate transformation and
empirical validity of a scale are therefore separate questions.
A.2. An example in which structural coordinates do not identify truth
Suppose the coding functionϕuses only specificity, dependency depth, and review level. “The
measurement in the source data is 3” and “The measurement in the source data is 4” can be
coded as single observational claims of the same form. If the source has one value, the statements
can sharez while differing in truth value.

principle, encode all information. The point concerns A/D/M coding with the meanings specified
in this paper, not the general possibility of mathematical encoding. Because that coding groups
different content states into the same observation coordinates, content and evidence must be
preserved.

running a tool receives an execution label; reading its result and incorporating it into a judgment
receives an incorporation label.

Appendix E. Pseudocode for Reference Selection and Acceptance
Contracts
The following pseudocode describes the combined design. The supplementary Python code
implements only scoring and selection, not the entire executor.
Input: problem, fixed goal/contracts, operator library,
cost budget, integer iteration limit
State <- initialize with source, evidence, goal, and versions
While iterations remain:
iteration limit <- iteration limit - 1
admissible set <- trusted executor 's precondition/permission checks
If the set is empty:
perform admissible recovery within budget; continue
if recovery is impossible, return indeterminate
U <- frozen shared baseline utility for all admissible operators
If weight is zero or observation/support/calibration is insufficient:
scores <- U; record the fallback reason
Else:
P, Q <- effect/expert distributions with the same category order
validate distributions; handle errors by predefined fallback/stop
for valid inputs, scores <- U - lambda_T * JS(P,Q)
operator <- highest score; ties use the unique bound-execution key
reserve costs for execution, checking, and necessary output checking
If the budget is insufficient, return indeterminate
candidate/artifacts <- execute in temporary state under finite timeout
verdict <- check input/candidate/artifact/contract/version bindings
account for actual selection, execution, and checking costs
If verdict is not pass:
retain accepted state; record event, reason, and cost
perform admissible recovery or return indeterminate
continue
State <- trusted executor applies only permitted writes
record realized and candidate coordinates separately
If a completion candidate exists:
output candidate <- construct from current accepted results
final verdict <- check goal obligations and output/version binding
account for cost; if pass, return exactly the checked output
otherwise perform admissible recovery or stop as indeterminate
Return incomplete/indeterminate when the iteration limit is exhausted
Distribution-format errors are recorded as numerical exceptions and separate failure reasons. A
service predetermines whether to fall back or stop. Contracts are not relaxed after an error to
reclassify it as success. A native-architecture version must actually include operator execution and
working-memory updates in forward or iterative latent computation, and is distinguished from a
version consisting only of external calls.

F.1. Input and numerical contracts of the reference code
reference_router.py is an arithmetic selector for arbitrary equal-length categorical distributions.
The paper’s default experiment uses the 93-category order intransition_alphabet.json: 90
pass combinations in nested A0-A4, D0-D4/D5+, M0-M2 order, followed by reject, unknown, and
stop. A compressed representation must preserve the same correspondence for all positive-mass
categories in both distributions.
admissible and trusted accept only Booleans; strings are not interpreted as permission or
trust judgments. Weights, utilities, and probabilities must be finite real numbers other than
Booleans. Distribution sums must be 1 within floating-point tolerance10−10, without automatic
renormalization. Zero-probability terms contribute 0, intermediate-mean underflow is avoided for
extremely small probabilities, and a nonfinite calculated score returns an error.
Proposition 6 in real arithmetic is distinct from checks of the floating-point implementation.
Random numerical checks use comparison tolerance10−12. Passing unit tests is not exhaustive
verification of every numerical input or of executor safety.operator_id is a unique selection key
whose version and arguments are bound externally; the module does not verify that binding or
actual write permissions.
